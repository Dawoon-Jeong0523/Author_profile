# Virtual committee support and reasoning: Macro

How to read: the virtual committee is the simulation described in the candidate notes. Each of its 33 ballots (11 personas x 3 models: A = claude-opus-5-5, O = gpt-5.5, G = gemini-3.1-pro) ranked up to five contributions in the field; rank 1 earns 5 points, rank 5 earns 1, each model's points are divided by its number of valid ballots and summed over the three models, so the score is at most 15 (every ballot of every model ranking the candidate first). Members = personas that nominated the candidate on at least one model. The reasoning summarizes the personas' rationales, including their reservations; it is simulated, not the real committee's view.

165 nominations in this field, integrated into 26 candidates; the 7 candidates of the pool hold 78 % of the field's total score.

1. Michael Woodford, Jordi Galí, Mark Gertler: for developing the New Keynesian framework of nominal rigidities and welfare-based monetary policy analysis
   Support: score 12.09 of 15 (A 4.91, O 5.00, G 2.18); 11 of 11 members; 31 nominations (A 11, O 11, G 9)
   Named in its nominations: Michael Woodford 29; Jordi Galí 22; Mark Gertler 14; also John B. Taylor 5; also Guillermo A. Calvo 4; also Julio J. Rotemberg 2
   Reasoning: Nominators see this as the workhorse framework of central banks, linking optimizing price setting, expectations and welfare-based interest-rate rules. It changed both research and policy practice. Reservations concern diffuse attribution (Clarida, Rotemberg, Calvo, Taylor) and the weak empirical fit of the Phillips curve and representative-agent core.

2. Nobuhiro Kiyotaki, John Moore: for showing how credit constraints and collateral amplify and propagate macroeconomic fluctuations
   Support: score 8.45 of 15 (A 3.36, O 2.27, G 2.82); 11 of 11 members; 26 nominations (A 10, O 7, G 9)
   Named in its nominations: Nobuhiro Kiyotaki 26; John Moore 26; also Mark Gertler 3
   Reasoning: A clean general-equilibrium mechanism in which asset prices and borrowing capacity feed back on each other became the core of post-2008 macro-finance models. It is widely seen as deep, general and tractable. Reservations are overlap with the 2022 Bernanke prize, debated quantitative size of amplification, and boundary with Finance.

3. Mark Huggett, Anthony A. Smith Jr., Truman Bewley: for the development of heterogeneous-agent incomplete-markets macroeconomics and methods for solving it with aggregate shocks
   Support: score 4.82 of 15 (A 0.45, O 2.91, G 1.45); 10 of 11 members; 16 nominations (A 2, O 10, G 4)
   Named in its nominations: Mark Huggett 11; Anthony A. Smith Jr. 9; Truman Bewley 5; also Per Krusell 14
   Named but not eligible: Per Krusell (member of the 2026 committee; cannot be awarded while serving)
   Reasoning: These models put uninsured idiosyncratic risk, wealth distributions and aggregate shocks into equilibrium, and the Krusell-Smith method made them quantitatively tractable; they are now standard for distributional policy analysis. The main reservation is attribution, since Aiyagari, a decisive contributor, is deceased.

4. John B. Taylor: for the formulation and analysis of rules for monetary policy and of staggered contracts
   Support: score 3.18 of 15 (A 1.73, O 1.09, G 0.36); 10 of 11 members; 16 nominations (A 7, O 6, G 3)
   Named in its nominations: John B. Taylor 16
   Reasoning: The Taylor rule is a rare case of research that directly reshaped how central banks describe and evaluate policy, and staggered contracts helped found nominal-rigidity models. It provides a transparent benchmark comparable across time and countries. Reservations are that it is a policy summary rather than a full structural theory and that it overlaps with the New Keynesian candidate and the 2004 prize.

5. Christina D. Romer, David H. Romer, Valerie A. Ramey: for the narrative identification of the macroeconomic effects of monetary and fiscal policy
   Support: score 2.36 of 15 (A 0.73, O 1.00, G 0.64); 6 of 11 members; 10 nominations (A 2, O 5, G 3)
   Named in its nominations: Christina D. Romer 6; David H. Romer 6; Valerie A. Ramey 8
   Reasoning: They built transparent, documented shock series from archival records and timing information, bringing credible design-based identification into macro policy evaluation. The series are open to replication and re-examination. Reservations are whether narrative shocks are truly exogenous and that multiplier estimates remain sensitive to specification.

6. Robert E. Hall: for the intertemporal analysis of consumption under rational expectations and of labor-market fluctuations
   Support: score 2.27 of 15 (A 1.27, O 0.00, G 1.00); 10 of 11 members; 14 nominations (A 10, O 0, G 4)
   Named in its nominations: Robert E. Hall 14; also Robert J. Barro 2
   Reasoning: The Euler-equation random-walk result turned consumption theory into a testable dynamic restriction and founded modern empirical consumption research. Hall's later work on wage stickiness sharpened the unemployment-volatility debate. Reservations are closeness to the Friedman and Modigliani prizes and a contribution spread across topics.

7. Robert J. Barro: for the analysis of public debt, Ricardian equivalence and tax smoothing in intertemporal macroeconomics
   Support: score 1.73 of 15 (A 1.27, O 0.09, G 0.36); 6 of 11 members; 8 nominations (A 6, O 1, G 1)
   Named in its nominations: Robert J. Barro 8
   Reasoning: Barro brought intertemporal optimization to fiscal policy and set the benchmark against which deficits and debt management are analysed, with tax smoothing a clean general result. Some nominations add his work on monetary credibility. Reservations are that strict Ricardian equivalence is rejected empirically and the credibility work overlaps the 2004 prize.
