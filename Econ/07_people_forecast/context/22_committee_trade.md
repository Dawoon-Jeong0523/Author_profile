# Virtual committee support and reasoning: Trade

How to read: the virtual committee is the simulation described in the candidate notes. Each of its 33 ballots (11 personas x 3 models: A = claude-opus-5-5, O = gpt-5.5, G = gemini-3.1-pro) ranked up to five contributions in the field; rank 1 earns 5 points, rank 5 earns 1, each model's points are divided by its number of valid ballots and summed over the three models, so the score is at most 15 (every ballot of every model ranking the candidate first). Members = personas that nominated the candidate on at least one model. The reasoning summarizes the personas' rationales, including their reservations; it is simulated, not the real committee's view.

165 nominations in this field, integrated into 18 candidates; the 7 candidates of the pool hold 94 % of the field's total score.

1. Marc J. Melitz, Andrew B. Bernard, Elhanan Helpman: for the analysis of firm heterogeneity, selection and reallocation in international trade
   Support: score 11.55 of 15 (A 2.55, O 5.00, G 4.00); 11 of 11 members; 30 nominations (A 8, O 11, G 11)
   Named in its nominations: Marc J. Melitz 30; Andrew B. Bernard 11; Elhanan Helpman 6; also J. Bradford Jensen 5; also Samuel Kortum 3; also Stephen J. Redding 2
   Named but not eligible: Jonathan Eaton (named in 2; died in 2024)
   Reasoning: Micro facts on exporters, documented by Bernard and Jensen, led to the Melitz model, which made selection and within-industry reallocation central to the gains from trade and became the field's standard workhorse. Nominators call it the clearest post-Krugman transformation of trade theory. The recurring reservation is Melitz's relative youth and how credit should be shared.

2. Samuel Kortum: for developing quantitative Ricardian general-equilibrium models of trade, technology and geography
   Support: score 7.27 of 15 (A 3.64, O 1.45, G 2.18); 11 of 11 members; 21 nominations (A 10, O 5, G 6)
   Named in its nominations: Samuel Kortum 21; also Marc J. Melitz 3
   Named but not eligible: Jonathan Eaton (named in 19; died in 2024)
   Reasoning: Eaton-Kortum turned Ricardian theory into a tractable, estimable general-equilibrium workhorse that underpins modern counterfactual trade-policy and welfare analysis. Nominators see it as a genuine bridge from theory to quantitative policy work. The main reservation is that welfare conclusions rest on strong distributional assumptions and on a few elasticities.

3. Gene M. Grossman, Elhanan Helpman: for the analysis of innovation, growth and the political economy of trade policy
   Support: score 7.09 of 15 (A 3.91, O 0.45, G 2.73); 11 of 11 members; 24 nominations (A 11, O 2, G 11)
   Named in its nominations: Gene M. Grossman 24; Elhanan Helpman 23
   Reasoning: Grossman and Helpman linked trade to endogenous innovation and modelled protection as the outcome of lobbying, building a broad and cumulative theoretical programme. Protection for Sale has been taken to data repeatedly. The reservations are its proximity to Krugman's 2008 prize, its breadth (which makes a sharp motivation hard to write) and empirical tests that rely on weak proxies.

4. Maurice Obstfeld, Kenneth Rogoff: for founding the micro-founded intertemporal new open-economy macroeconomics
   Support: score 4.91 of 15 (A 2.73, O 1.45, G 0.73); 11 of 11 members; 24 nominations (A 11, O 9, G 4)
   Named in its nominations: Maurice Obstfeld 24; Kenneth Rogoff 24
   Reasoning: They brought dynamic optimizing general equilibrium with nominal rigidities into open-economy macroeconomics, replacing Mundell-Fleming as the standard framework for welfare-based policy analysis. The reservation, raised almost uniformly, is that exchange-rate models still perform poorly empirically. Producer-currency pricing was also later contradicted by the invoicing evidence.

5. David H. Autor, David Dorn, Gordon H. Hanson: for the empirical analysis of the local labor-market effects of import competition
   Support: score 4.55 of 15 (A 0.64, O 1.36, G 2.55); 8 of 11 members; 16 nominations (A 3, O 6, G 7)
   Named in its nominations: David H. Autor 16; David Dorn 14; Gordon H. Hanson 14
   Reasoning: The China-shock work used credible regional exposure designs to show large, persistent and geographically concentrated adjustment costs of trade, and it reshaped both research and the policy debate. The findings have largely replicated in direction. The reservations are debates over shift-share identification, general-equilibrium offsets, the external validity of a single episode, and maturity.

6. Pol Antràs, Gene M. Grossman, Esteban Rossi-Hansberg: for the analysis of offshoring, incomplete contracts and the organization of global value chains
   Support: score 4.00 of 15 (A 0.18, O 2.18, G 1.64); 11 of 11 members; 20 nominations (A 1, O 11, G 8)
   Named in its nominations: Pol Antràs 20; Gene M. Grossman 10; Esteban Rossi-Hansberg 7; also Elhanan Helpman 3
   Reasoning: This work made firm boundaries and global value chains endogenous to contracting frictions and introduced the trade-in-tasks view of offshoring. Nominators value the link between trade, organization and institutions. The reservations are that empirical measurement of value chains is less settled, that part of the underlying contract theory has been recognized elsewhere, and Antràs's youth.

7. James E. Anderson, Eric van Wincoop: for the theoretical foundation of structural gravity and the measurement of trade costs
   Support: score 2.73 of 15 (A 0.09, O 2.64, G 0.00); 10 of 11 members; 10 nominations (A 1, O 9, G 0)
   Named in its nominations: James E. Anderson 10; Eric van Wincoop 10
   Reasoning: They grounded the most robust empirical regularity in trade in theory and corrected its estimation through multilateral resistance, creating a structural tool for counterfactual trade-policy analysis. The reservation is that gravity is seen as a method and measurement infrastructure rather than a single substantive mechanism.
