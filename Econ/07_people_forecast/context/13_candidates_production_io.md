# Candidates for the 2026 Prize in Economic Sciences: Production, Industrial Organization

Field: Production, Industrial Organization (JEL D2, L, M): theory of the firm, transaction costs and organizations, industrial organization and regulation, business economics. Field forecast: 20.2 % of the five fields of the pool (note 01).

The 6 candidates below are combinations of one to three people with the contribution a prize would recognize. They were nominated by a simulated committee (eleven personas reconstructed from the published research records of the members of the 2026 prize committee, each asked on three language models without web access) and integrated across the models; the defining works are the publications the nominations cite as establishing the contribution. The list is a candidate set, not a statement of the real committee members' views. Previous laureates and members of the 2026 committee are not included; the living status of the people was screened on Wikidata, and people named by the committee who have died are noted and left out of the lineups.

1. Steven Berry (b. 1959), James Levinsohn (b. 1958), Ariel Pakes (b. 1949)
   Contribution: for developing structural methods for the empirical analysis of demand and market power in differentiated-product markets [L13]
   Defining works:
   - Berry, Levinsohn and Pakes, 1995: Automobile Prices in Market Equilibrium, Econometrica
   - Berry, 1994: Estimating Discrete-Choice Models of Product Differentiation, RAND Journal of Economics
   - Berry, Levinsohn and Pakes, 2004: Differentiated Products Demand Systems from a Combination of Micro and Macro Data, Journal of Political Economy
   - Olley and Pakes, 1996: The Dynamics of Productivity in the Telecommunications Equipment Industry, Econometrica

2. Nicholas Bloom (b. 1973), John Van Reenen (b. 1965), Raffaella Sadun (b. 1974)
   Contribution: for measuring management practices and their effects on productivity across firms and countries [M11]
   Defining works:
   - Bloom and Van Reenen, 2007: Measuring and Explaining Management Practices Across Firms and Countries, Quarterly Journal of Economics
   - Bloom, Eifert, Mahajan, McKenzie and Roberts, 2013: Does Management Matter? Evidence from India, Quarterly Journal of Economics
   - Bloom, Sadun and Van Reenen, 2012: Americans Do IT Better: US Multinationals and the Productivity Miracle, American Economic Review

3. John Sutton (b. 1948)
   Contribution: for the analysis of endogenous sunk costs and the bounds approach to market structure [L11]
   Defining works:
   - Sutton, 1991: Sunk Costs and Market Structure, MIT Press
   - Sutton, 1998: Technology and Market Structure, MIT Press
   - Sutton, 2007: Market Structure: Theory and Evidence, in Handbook of Industrial Organization

4. Jean-Charles Rochet (b. 1957), Mark Armstrong (b. 1964), Bruno Jullien (b. 1961)
   Contribution: for the theory of two-sided markets and platform competition [L14]
   Defining works:
   - Rochet and Tirole, 2003: Platform Competition in Two-Sided Markets, Journal of the European Economic Association
   - Armstrong, 2006: Competition in Two-Sided Markets, RAND Journal of Economics
   - Caillaud and Jullien, 2003: Chicken and Egg: Competition among Intermediation Service Providers, RAND Journal of Economics
   - Rochet and Tirole, 2006: Two-Sided Markets: A Progress Report, RAND Journal of Economics

5. John C. Haltiwanger (b. 1955), Lucia Foster, Chad Syverson (b. 1972)
   Contribution: for the empirical analysis of productivity dispersion, firm dynamics and reallocation using microdata [L25]
   Defining works:
   - Foster, Haltiwanger and Krizan, 2001: Aggregate Productivity Growth: Lessons from Microeconomic Evidence, in New Developments in Productivity Analysis
   - Foster, Haltiwanger and Syverson, 2008: Reallocation, Firm Turnover, and Efficiency: Selection on Productivity or Profitability?, American Economic Review
   - Davis, Haltiwanger and Schuh, 1996: Job Creation and Destruction, MIT Press
   - Syverson, 2011: What Determines Productivity?, Journal of Economic Literature

6. Timothy F. Bresnahan (b. 1953), Peter C. Reiss (b. 1957)
   Contribution: for the empirical analysis of entry and competition in concentrated markets [L11]
   Defining works:
   - Bresnahan and Reiss, 1991: Entry and Competition in Concentrated Markets, Journal of Political Economy
   - Bresnahan and Reiss, 1990: Entry in Monopoly Markets, Review of Economic Studies
