# Virtual committee support and reasoning: Public, Law, Political Economy

How to read: the virtual committee is the simulation described in the candidate notes. Each of its 33 ballots (11 personas x 3 models: A = claude-opus-5-5, O = gpt-5.5, G = gemini-3.1-pro) ranked up to five contributions in the field; rank 1 earns 5 points, rank 5 earns 1, each model's points are divided by its number of valid ballots and summed over the three models, so the score is at most 15 (every ballot of every model ranking the candidate first). Members = personas that nominated the candidate on at least one model. The reasoning summarizes the personas' rationales, including their reservations; it is simulated, not the real committee's view.

165 nominations in this field, integrated into 22 candidates; the 6 candidates of the pool hold 73 % of the field's total score.

1. Torsten Persson, Guido Tabellini: for the analysis of how political institutions and constitutions shape economic and fiscal policy
   Support: score 10.36 of 15 (A 4.45, O 3.55, G 2.36); 11 of 11 members; 30 nominations (A 11, O 11, G 8)
   Named in its nominations: Torsten Persson 30; Guido Tabellini 30; also Timothy Besley 4
   Reasoning: Nominators credit Persson and Tabellini with making political economics a rigorous field of equilibrium policy models confronted with cross-country evidence on electoral rules and constitutions. The recurring reservation is fragile cross-country identification of constitutional effects, plus proximity to the 2024 institutions prize and Persson's IIES ties and past committee role.

2. Emmanuel Saez, Raj Chetty, Henrik Kleven: for linking optimal tax and social insurance theory to empirically estimable sufficient statistics
   Support: score 5.55 of 15 (A 1.64, O 2.73, G 1.18); 8 of 11 members; 14 nominations (A 5, O 6, G 3)
   Named in its nominations: Emmanuel Saez 13; Raj Chetty 12; Henrik Kleven 6; also Thomas Piketty 3
   Reasoning: Nominators credit this work with making normative tax and insurance theory testable through elasticities estimated from administrative data and designs such as bunching. Reservations are the youth of the candidates, the breadth of contributors, and that sufficient statistics can miss general-equilibrium feedback and elasticities vary across settings.

3. Steven Shavell, Richard A. Posner, Guido Calabresi: for the economic analysis of law, liability and enforcement
   Support: score 5.36 of 15 (A 2.18, O 2.82, G 0.36); 11 of 11 members; 21 nominations (A 9, O 11, G 1)

4. Emmanuel Saez, Thomas Piketty, Gabriel Zucman: for the measurement of top incomes and wealth from tax records and its integration into the theory of optimal taxation
   Support: score 4.64 of 15 (A 2.45, O 2.18, G 0.00); 8 of 11 members; 13 nominations (A 7, O 6, G 0)
   Named in its nominations: Emmanuel Saez 13; Thomas Piketty 13; Gabriel Zucman 4

Reservations are measurement disputes, contested elasticities, the candidates' youth and the need to separate science from advocacy.
