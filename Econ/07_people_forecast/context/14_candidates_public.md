# Candidates for the 2026 Prize in Economic Sciences: Public, Law, Political Economy

Field: Public, Law, Political Economy (JEL D7, H, K, P): public economics and taxation, collective decision making and public choice, law and economics, political economy and economic systems. Field forecast: 19.2 % of the five fields of the pool (note 01).

The 6 candidates below are combinations of one to three people with the contribution a prize would recognize. They were nominated by a simulated committee (eleven personas reconstructed from the published research records of the members of the 2026 prize committee, each asked on three language models without web access) and integrated across the models; the defining works are the publications the nominations cite as establishing the contribution. The list is a candidate set, not a statement of the real committee members' views. Previous laureates and members of the 2026 committee are not included; the living status of the people was screened on Wikidata, and people named by the committee who have died are noted and left out of the lineups.

1. Torsten Persson (b. 1954), Guido Tabellini (b. 1956)
   Contribution: for the analysis of how political institutions and constitutions shape economic and fiscal policy [D72]
   Defining works:
   - Persson and Tabellini, 2000: Political Economics: Explaining Economic Policy, MIT Press
   - Persson and Tabellini, 2003: The Economic Effects of Constitutions, MIT Press
   - Persson and Tabellini, 1994: Is Inequality Harmful for Growth?, American Economic Review
   - Persson, Roland and Tabellini, 1997: Separation of Powers and Political Accountability, Quarterly Journal of Economics

2. Emmanuel Saez (b. 1972), Raj Chetty (b. 1979), Henrik Kleven (b. 1973)
   Contribution: for linking optimal tax and social insurance theory to empirically estimable sufficient statistics [H21]
   Defining works:
   - Saez, 2001: Using Elasticities to Derive Optimal Income Tax Rates, Review of Economic Studies
   - Chetty, 2009: Sufficient Statistics for Welfare Analysis, Annual Review of Economics
   - Kleven and Waseem, 2013: Using notches to uncover optimization frictions and structural elasticities, Quarterly Journal of Economics
   - Kleven, Knudsen, Kreiner, Pedersen and Saez, 2011: "Unwilling or Unable to Cheat? Evidence from a Tax Audit Experiment in Denmark", Econometrica

3. Steven Shavell (b. 1946), Richard A. Posner (b. 1939), Guido Calabresi (b. 1932)
   Contribution: for the economic analysis of law, liability and enforcement [K13]
   Defining works:
   - Posner, 1973: Economic Analysis of Law, Little, Brown
   - Calabresi, 1970: The Costs of Accidents, Yale University Press
   - Shavell, 1987: Economic Analysis of Accident Law, Harvard University Press
   - Polinsky and Shavell, 1979: The Optimal Tradeoff between the Probability and Magnitude of Fines, American Economic Review

4. Emmanuel Saez (b. 1972), Thomas Piketty (b. 1971), Gabriel Zucman (b. 1986)
   Contribution: for the measurement of top incomes and wealth from tax records and its integration into the theory of optimal taxation [H24]
   Defining works:
   - Piketty and Saez, 2003: Income Inequality in the United States, 1913-1998, Quarterly Journal of Economics
   - Piketty, Saez and Stantcheva, 2014: Optimal Taxation of Top Labor Incomes, American Economic Journal: Economic Policy
   - Saez and Zucman, 2016: Wealth Inequality in the United States since 1913, Quarterly Journal of Economics
   - Piketty, Saez and Zucman, 2018: "Distributional National Accounts: Methods and Estimates for the United States", Quarterly Journal of Economics

5. Andrei Shleifer (b. 1961), Rafael La Porta (b. 1960), Florencio Lopez-de-Silanes (b. 1964)
   Contribution: for showing how legal origins and investor protection shape financial and economic development [K22]
   Defining works:
   - La Porta, Lopez-de-Silanes, Shleifer and Vishny, 1998: Law and Finance, Journal of Political Economy
   - La Porta, Lopez-de-Silanes and Shleifer, 2008: The Economic Consequences of Legal Origins, Journal of Economic Literature
   - La Porta, Lopez-de-Silanes, Shleifer and Vishny, 1997: Legal Determinants of External Finance, Journal of Finance

6. Amy Finkelstein (b. 1973), Jonathan Gruber (b. 1965), Raj Chetty (b. 1979)
   Contribution: for the empirical analysis of health insurance and social insurance design [H51]
   Defining works:
   - Finkelstein, 2007: The Aggregate Effects of Health Insurance: Evidence from the Introduction of Medicare, Quarterly Journal of Economics
   - Gruber, 1994: The Incidence of Mandated Maternity Benefits, American Economic Review
   - Finkelstein, Taubman, Wright, Bernstein, Gruber, Newhouse, Allen and Baicker, 2012: The Oregon Health Insurance Experiment: Evidence from the First Year, Quarterly Journal of Economics
   - Chetty, 2006: A General Formula for the Optimal Level of Social Insurance, Journal of Public Economics
