# Candidates for the 2026 Prize in Economic Sciences: Equilibrium, Welfare

Field: Equilibrium, Welfare (JEL C6, D5, D6): general equilibrium, welfare economics, social choice, mathematical and optimization methods. Field forecast: 13.7 % of the five fields of the pool (note 01).

The 4 candidates below are combinations of one to three people with the contribution a prize would recognize. They were nominated by a simulated committee (eleven personas reconstructed from the published research records of the members of the 2026 prize committee, each asked on three language models without web access) and integrated across the models; the defining works are the publications the nominations cite as establishing the contribution. The list is a candidate set, not a statement of the real committee members' views. Previous laureates and members of the 2026 committee are not included; the living status of the people was screened on Wikidata, and people named by the committee who have died are noted and left out of the lineups.

1. Andreu Mas-Colell (b. 1944), Yves Balasko (b. 1945)
   Contribution: for the analysis of the structure, regularity and limits of general equilibrium theory [D51]
   Note: Hugo Sonnenschein, named by the committee for this contribution, died in 2021 and is not eligible.
   Defining works:
   - Sonnenschein, 1973: Do Walras' Identity and Continuity Characterize the Class of Community Excess Demand Functions?, Journal of Economic Theory
   - Mas-Colell, 1985: The Theory of General Economic Equilibrium: A Differentiable Approach, Cambridge University Press
   - Sonnenschein, 1972: Market Excess Demand Functions, Econometrica
   - Balasko, 1975: Some Results on Uniqueness and on Stability of Equilibrium in General Equilibrium Theory, Journal of Mathematical Economics

2. John E. Roemer (b. 1945), Marc Fleurbaey (b. 1961), François Maniquet (b. 1965)
   Contribution: for the theory of equality of opportunity, responsibility and fairness in welfare economics [D63]
   Defining works:
   - Roemer, 1998: Equality of Opportunity, Harvard University Press
   - Fleurbaey, 2008: Fairness, Responsibility, and Welfare, Oxford University Press
   - Fleurbaey and Maniquet, 2011: A Theory of Fairness and Social Welfare, Cambridge University Press

3. John Geanakoplos (b. 1955), Herakles Polemarchakis (b. 1952), Michael Magill (b. 1942)
   Contribution: for the theory of general equilibrium with incomplete markets and its constrained inefficiency [D52]
   Defining works:
   - Geanakoplos and Polemarchakis, 1986: Existence, Regularity, and Constrained Suboptimality of Competitive Allocations When the Asset Market Is Incomplete, in Essays in Honor of Kenneth Arrow
   - Magill and Quinzii, 1996: Theory of Incomplete Markets, MIT Press
   - Geanakoplos, 2010: The Leverage Cycle, NBER Macroeconomics Annual

4. Partha Dasgupta (b. 1942), Geoffrey Heal (b. 1944)
   Contribution: for the welfare economics of sustainability, natural capital and intergenerational wealth accounting [D60]
   Defining works:
   - Dasgupta and Heal, 1979: Economic Theory and Exhaustible Resources, Cambridge University Press
   - Dasgupta, 2021: The Economics of Biodiversity: The Dasgupta Review, HM Treasury
   - Dasgupta, 2001: Human Well-Being and the Natural Environment, Oxford University Press
   - Arrow, Dasgupta, Goulder et al., 2004: Are We Consuming Too Much?, Journal of Economic Perspectives
