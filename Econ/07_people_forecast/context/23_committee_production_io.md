# Virtual committee support and reasoning: Production, Industrial Organization

How to read: the virtual committee is the simulation described in the candidate notes. Each of its 33 ballots (11 personas x 3 models: A = claude-opus-5-5, O = gpt-5.5, G = gemini-3.1-pro) ranked up to five contributions in the field; rank 1 earns 5 points, rank 5 earns 1, each model's points are divided by its number of valid ballots and summed over the three models, so the score is at most 15 (every ballot of every model ranking the candidate first). Members = personas that nominated the candidate on at least one model. The reasoning summarizes the personas' rationales, including their reservations; it is simulated, not the real committee's view.

165 nominations in this field, integrated into 28 candidates; the 6 candidates of the pool hold 77 % of the field's total score.

1. Steven Berry, James Levinsohn, Ariel Pakes: for developing structural methods for the empirical analysis of demand and market power in differentiated-product markets
   Support: score 14.45 of 15 (A 5.00, O 5.00, G 4.45); 11 of 11 members; 33 nominations (A 11, O 11, G 11)
   Named in its nominations: Steven Berry 33; James Levinsohn 26; Ariel Pakes 33
   Reasoning: BLP is described as the workhorse of empirical IO, used daily in merger review and antitrust worldwide, combining equilibrium modelling with estimation and with clear attribution. The recurring reservation is sensitivity to instruments, conduct and computational assumptions, and closeness to econometrics.

2. Nicholas Bloom, John Van Reenen, Raffaella Sadun: for measuring management practices and their effects on productivity across firms and countries
   Support: score 7.45 of 15 (A 2.73, O 1.36, G 3.36); 11 of 11 members; 26 nominations (A 11, O 6, G 9)
   Named in its nominations: Nicholas Bloom 26; John Van Reenen 26; Raffaella Sadun 6
   Reasoning: The World Management Survey created durable measurement infrastructure, backed by a field experiment, linking management to productivity dispersion. The main reservations are the youth of the candidates, survey-based measurement and limited causal settings.

3. John Sutton: for the analysis of endogenous sunk costs and the bounds approach to market structure
   Support: score 4.82 of 15 (A 1.91, O 1.82, G 1.09); 8 of 11 members; 18 nominations (A 7, O 5, G 6)
   Named in its nominations: John Sutton 18
   Reasoning: Sutton derived robust bounds on concentration that hold across game forms and explained why concentration need not fall as markets grow, linking theory to cross-industry evidence. The main reservation is that his influence on current empirical and policy practice is smaller than that of structural IO.

5. John C. Haltiwanger, Lucia Foster, Chad Syverson: for the empirical analysis of productivity dispersion, firm dynamics and reallocation using microdata
   Support: score 2.00 of 15 (A 0.00, O 1.91, G 0.09); 7 of 11 members; 8 nominations (A 0, O 7, G 1)
