# Candidates for the 2026 Prize in Economic Sciences: Macro

Field: Macro (JEL E): business cycles, money and monetary policy, consumption and saving, fiscal policy, national accounts. Field forecast: 24.4 % of the five fields of the pool (note 01).

The 7 candidates below are combinations of one to three people with the contribution a prize would recognize. They were nominated by a simulated committee (eleven personas reconstructed from the published research records of the members of the 2026 prize committee, each asked on three language models without web access) and integrated across the models; the defining works are the publications the nominations cite as establishing the contribution. The list is a candidate set, not a statement of the real committee members' views. Previous laureates and members of the 2026 committee are not included; the living status of the people was screened on Wikidata, and people named by the committee who have died are noted and left out of the lineups.

1. Michael Woodford (b. 1955), Jordi Galí (b. 1961), Mark Gertler (b. 1951)
   Contribution: for developing the New Keynesian framework of nominal rigidities and welfare-based monetary policy analysis [E52]
   Defining works:
   - Woodford, 2003: Interest and Prices, Princeton University Press
   - Clarida, Galí and Gertler, 1999: The Science of Monetary Policy: A New Keynesian Perspective, Journal of Economic Literature
   - Galí and Gertler, 1999: Inflation Dynamics: A Structural Econometric Analysis, Journal of Monetary Economics
   - Galí, 2008: Monetary Policy, Inflation, and the Business Cycle, Princeton University Press

2. Nobuhiro Kiyotaki (b. 1955), John Moore (b. 1954)
   Contribution: for showing how credit constraints and collateral amplify and propagate macroeconomic fluctuations [E44]
   Defining works:
   - Kiyotaki and Moore, 1997: Credit Cycles, Journal of Political Economy
   - Kiyotaki and Moore, 2002: Balance-Sheet Contagion, American Economic Review Papers and Proceedings

3. Mark Huggett (b. 1959), Anthony A. Smith Jr. (b. 1962), Truman Bewley (b. 1941)
   Contribution: for the development of heterogeneous-agent incomplete-markets macroeconomics and methods for solving it with aggregate shocks [E21]
   Defining works:
   - Krusell and Smith, 1998: Income and Wealth Heterogeneity in the Macroeconomy, Journal of Political Economy
   - Huggett, 1993: The Risk-Free Rate in Heterogeneous-Agent Incomplete-Insurance Economies, Journal of Economic Dynamics and Control
   - Bewley, 1986: Stationary Monetary Equilibrium with a Continuum of Independently Fluctuating Consumers, in Contributions to Mathematical Economics

4. John B. Taylor (b. 1946)
   Contribution: for the formulation and analysis of rules for monetary policy and of staggered contracts [E52]
   Defining works:
   - Taylor, 1993: Discretion versus Policy Rules in Practice, Carnegie-Rochester Conference Series on Public Policy
   - Taylor, 1980: Aggregate Dynamics and Staggered Contracts, Journal of Political Economy
   - Taylor, 1999: A Historical Analysis of Monetary Policy Rules, in Monetary Policy Rules, University of Chicago Press

5. Christina D. Romer (b. 1958), David H. Romer (b. 1958), Valerie A. Ramey (b. 1959)
   Contribution: for the narrative identification of the macroeconomic effects of monetary and fiscal policy [E62]
   Defining works:
   - Romer and Romer, 2004: A New Measure of Monetary Shocks: Derivation and Implications, American Economic Review
   - Romer and Romer, 2010: The Macroeconomic Effects of Tax Changes: Estimates Based on a New Measure of Fiscal Shocks, American Economic Review
   - Ramey, 2011: Identifying Government Spending Shocks: It's All in the Timing, Quarterly Journal of Economics
   - Romer and Romer, 1989: Does Monetary Policy Matter? A New Test in the Spirit of Friedman and Schwartz, NBER Macroeconomics Annual

6. Robert E. Hall (b. 1943)
   Contribution: for the intertemporal analysis of consumption under rational expectations and of labor-market fluctuations [E21]
   Defining works:
   - Hall, 1978: Stochastic Implications of the Life Cycle-Permanent Income Hypothesis, Journal of Political Economy
   - Hall, 2005: Employment Fluctuations with Equilibrium Wage Stickiness, American Economic Review

7. Robert J. Barro (b. 1944)
   Contribution: for the analysis of public debt, Ricardian equivalence and tax smoothing in intertemporal macroeconomics [E62]
   Defining works:
   - Barro, 1974: Are Government Bonds Net Wealth?, Journal of Political Economy
   - Barro, 1979: On the Determination of the Public Debt, Journal of Political Economy
   - Barro and Gordon, 1983: Rules, Discretion and Reputation in a Model of Monetary Policy, Journal of Monetary Economics
