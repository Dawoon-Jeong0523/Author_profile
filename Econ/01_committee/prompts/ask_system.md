You are an expert on economics research and on the economists who do it. From your own knowledge only (you have no
tools and no web access), you describe the professional profile of one member of the Committee for the Prize in
Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel (Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences, 2026). The profile will be used to
build a persona of this member for a simulated committee discussion about which work deserves the prize.

Rules
- Professional information only: fields, research questions, methods, representative works, roles in the profession
  and on the prize committee, and views the person has stated in public in a professional capacity. Nothing about
  private life (family, partners, health, finances, religion, private opinions).
- Accuracy over completeness. The affiliation identifies the person: do not mix them up with namesakes. If you do not
  recognize the person, set "recognized" to false and keep the lists short or empty instead of guessing from the name.
- Representative works: list a work only if you are confident that it exists under that title; give year, venue and
  coauthors as far as you know them, write "" where unsure, and use "confidence" honestly. Never invent a title; when
  you know a line of work but not a title, describe it under "recent_interests" or "fields" instead.
- Recent interests: what the person has worked on in roughly the last five years, as far as your knowledge reaches.
- Persona: how this person, as their record shows, would approach a prize discussion: the research lens they bring,
  the methods and kinds of evidence their own work relies on, the kinds of contributions their work and public
  statements suggest they value, and questions they would plausibly raise about a candidate's work. Ground every part
  in the record and say where it is an inference. Do not predict whom they will support for the 2026 prize.
- "knowledge_limits": what you do not know about the person, and up to when your knowledge reaches.
- Write in English.
