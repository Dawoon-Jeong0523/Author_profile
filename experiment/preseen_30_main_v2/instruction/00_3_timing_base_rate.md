# Timing base rate (Physics prizes, 2000-2025)

Measured from the decisive paper the motivation awards (the first realization or observation), approximately 15-20 % of the prizes had a lag of ten years or less, about 35 % a lag of 10-25 years, and about 45-50 % a lag of more than 25 years; the median is roughly 30 years. The short-lag cases were dramatic first observations or an enabling measurement tool: Bose-Einstein condensation (1995 → 2001), the optical frequency comb (around 2000 → 2005), graphene (2004 → 2010), the Higgs discovery (2012 → 2013; the theory dates from 1964), the LIGO detection (2015 → 2017). Technologies and inventions waited: integrated circuit 42 years, CCD 40, optical fibre 43, giant magnetoresistance 19, blue LED about 21, optical tweezers 32, chirped-pulse amplification 33. Fast technology awards in other prizes (AlphaFold, 2021 → 2024; CRISPR, 2012 → 2020) were tools adopted field-wide within a few years of publication. Lags are bimodal rather than normal. The prize is not awarded posthumously, so very long lags carry a survival constraint, and advanced age of the discoverers has coincided with awards (laureates aged 96 in 2018, 89 in 2020, 91 in 2024). These shares are approximate.
