# Definitions used in the profile notes

Each profile note describes one person named in an option, from the OpenAlex 2026-01 snapshot (works, citations, book citations), PatentsView 2025-12-31 (US patents) and Reliance on Science (patent-to-paper citations). The window is the person's career from age 25: works published, and US utility patents filed, from the year the person turned 25 up to 2021 (patents granted by 2021). Birth years come from Wikidata and published biographies; where none is published, the year is estimated from the education record and the note says "born c.". Earlier works are left out because merged author records often carry a namesake's early papers.

- Research works: articles, reviews and letters.
- Impact percentile: a work's 5-year citation count compared with all OpenAlex works of the same publication year and field: 0 = lowest, 0.5 = typical, 1 = highest. "Top 10 %" / "top 1 %" = percentile at least 0.90 / 0.99. Impact measures include works with any number of authors, including large-collaboration papers.
- Disruption percentile: the 5-year disruption index (CD) of a work, as a percentile of the same cohort: high when the works citing it do not also cite its references. Works without references have no value.
- Foundation share: the share of the works citing a work within 5 years that build on the work itself rather than on its references.
- Citing inventions: distinct inventions (US patents and pre-grant publications) whose front-page or in-text references cite the person's works of the window; own patents: US utility patents with the person as an inventor, filed in the window.
- Textbook reach: citations from books, book chapters and reference entries to the works of the window; "citing books" counts distinct books.
- Nobel laureate co-authors: co-authors on works of the window with at most 50 authors who are Nobel laureates in physics, chemistry or physiology or medicine, with the number of such shared works; people on both sides: people who are both a co-author and a co-inventor in the window.
- Defining works: up to three research works of the window that are most tied to the discovery of the option the person is listed under, chosen by a language-model review of the person's 80 most-cited works of the window (landmark originals before reviews and later refinements). They are selected by discovery relevance, not by citation count; a slot with no discovery-related work is filled with the person's most-cited remaining work. A work missing from the metrics tables, or credited to the person by OpenAlex after its January 2026 snapshot, is listed without impact, disruption and Foundation values.
- Reference lines compare a person's value with the 62 Physics laureates of 2000-2025 measured at the time of their prize with the same age-25 window: only their works published from the year they turned 25 to the year before the prize, and only patent and book citations dated before the prize (undated works and citations left out). The person's own values cover the window up to 2021, with citations counted to the 2026 snapshot, so cumulative counts (patent and book citations) are measured with more elapsed time than those of early-2000s laureates. These are descriptive bibliometric measures, not forecasts.

# Option-level fields

- Option summary line: the number of named people; the mean and maximum across them of each measure (research works, median impact percentile, top-10 % and top-1 % shares, citing inventions, own patents, works cited by books, citing books); and the defining or anchor works shared with other options.
- Anchor works, where the named people have them: the works to which the option's discovery is attributed — the first discovery or prediction paper and the first realization or observation paper — with the same per-work measures as defining works, each labelled "discovery/prediction" or "realization/observation". Selected by discovery relevance, not citation count, from the works of the option's named people.
- Maturity line: years from each anchor work to 2026 (where an OpenAlex record is a later reprint, from the first publication), to be read against the timing base rate.
- Attribution confidence: high, medium or low, with the reason (for example an estimated birth year, an author record that misses the person's landmark works, or an author record merged by hand).
