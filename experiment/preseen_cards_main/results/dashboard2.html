Overview

In the first experiment (Dashboard 1) the profile cards, attached as context to be considered, moved Preseen’s forecasts less than repeated runs without them differ from each other, and the forecaster said it used them only as a secondary cross-check. Here the same cards go onto new copies of the same questions together with one more note, attached as an assumption to apply: the profiles are the main evidence for comparing the named options. One new control run and one run with the instruction were made per field on 2 October 2026 and compared with the five control runs and the three cross-check runs. A fourth arm, added the same day, softens the note: the profiles are one of the main sources of evidence, weighed comparably with prizes, news, predictions and the prize’s history, which the forecaster is asked to use actively (one run per field).

How the one-main-source run used the cards

Summary of the reasoning

The run weighs profiles and awards side by side. It states that the profiles get "influence comparable to awards and other external evidence", then runs three checks: whether orexin's 2026 milestones are substantive, whether the profiles change a headline-led ranking, and whether omitted discoveries justify a large "Other". A base-rate audit frames the awards: 18 of 26 Medicine prizes since 2000 went to a team with a prior Lasker, but only 1 of 25 Lasker Basic awards matched the same year's Nobel, so a fresh Lasker is read as readiness, not as an imminent prize.

Orexin leads at 11.7 % (control 12.1 %). The case combines its defining papers (de Lecea's hypocretin paper: disruption percentile 0.94, foundation share 0.86) with the 2026 Lasker and the FDA approval of an orexin-targeting drug in August 2026.

GLP-1 halves but survives: 20.3 % → 11.0 % (main-evidence run: 3.0 %). Holst's inventions and textbook reach are called exceptional, but the team's career-wide impact comparisons are "less uniformly exceptional than those of several competing teams"; the dense award record (Lasker 2024, Breakthrough 2025, Clarivate 2026) keeps it second.

Profile-strong options gain modestly: CAR-T 6.0 → 8.6 %, Wnt/organoids 2.3 → 5.4 %, leptin 1.9 → 3.5 %, PCSK9 2.9 → 4.1 %; the unfolded protein response falls 5.8 → 3.5 %. "Other" is 34 %.

How the main-evidence run used the cards

Summary of the reasoning

The run turns the cards into a scoring model. The main-evidence run scores every named person from the card figures (impact, technological translation, textbook reach and collaboration weighted 40/30/25/5), blends the strongest person with the group mean (65:35), adds the option's defining works (20 %), and averages 81 weightings with a 10 % uniform component. It says it accepts the premises "without a vintage or attribution penalty". Prizes and news no longer rank the named options; they are used to size "Other" (40 %), which now lists optical coherence tomography, integrins, cGAS, TOR and low-complexity domains as unlisted contenders.

The award favourite collapses. GLP-1 falls from 20.3 % (control mean) to 3.0 %: the write-up cites Holst's translation figures but finds the group's impact comparisons and Mojsov's and Knudsen's book reach "below the leaders". Its 2024 Lasker and the 2026 Clarivate selection are mentioned but no longer move it. Orexin, the other award-backed option, falls from 12.1 % to 4.8 %.

Profile-strong options rise. Optogenetics (7.9 %; Deisseroth with 37 % of works in the cohort top 1 %, 103 patents, 3,009 citing books) and Wnt/organoids (7.7 %; Clevers with 2,724 citing inventions) lead the named options, followed by inherited breast-cancer genes, leptin and PCSK9 (5.7–6.4 % each). The named options now lie between 2 % and 8 % instead of led by one or two favourites.

The same-day control run is unchanged: GLP-1 18 %, orexin 12 %, Other 32.9 %, as on 1 October.

Context

This forecast uses information available by October 2, 2026, at 15:15 UTC. The Medicine announcement is scheduled for October 5, 2026, at 11:30 CEST at the earliest, equivalent to 09:30 UTC; the result therefore remains ahead of the client's submission time (official schedule). I apply the client's discovery-based resolution rule. A different laureate roster does not make a matching discovery resolve to Other. A divided award is governed by the specified share and announcement-order rules.

The supplied profiles are a main evidence source, with influence comparable to awards and other external evidence. Their stated vintages are OpenAlex January 2026 and PatentsView December 31, 2025, covering works and patent grants through 2021. Their historical reference population is 60 Medicine laureates from 2000–2025, measured at prize time. I accept these figures as premises. Impact and disruption measures are dimensionless percentiles; foundation shares are proportions; inventions, patents and books are counts. References labeled supplied profiles identify these client-provided premises, not independently collected measurements.

Evidence

The historical backbone supports using major awards to identify Nobel-class discoveries, but not to identify the winning year. A person-matching audit of all 26 Medicine Nobel award years from 2000 through 2025 found that 18 contained a laureate with a prior or same-year Lasker Basic or Clinical award: 69.2%. Public Service and Special Achievement awards were excluded. This is a calculated proportion of Nobel-winning teams, not a subsequent winning probability for Lasker recipients (Nobel archive, Lasker archive).

Same-year overlap was much rarer. Across the complete 2000–2025 window, only one of the 25 actual Lasker Basic award groups overlapped that year's Medicine Nobel: 4%. The missing award year was a documented cancellation, not missing data. The sole match was Südhof's work on neurotransmitter release, which formed part of the Nobel's broader vesicle-traffic discovery. These counts use official award-year records available at the forecast cutoff (cancellation statement, Lasker discovery, Nobel motivation). I therefore treat a fresh Lasker as evidence of readiness, not a near-certain signal of an imminent Nobel.

Three checks drove the assessment: whether orexin's recent milestones were substantive rather than publicity; whether the profiles changed a headline-led ranking; and whether omitted discoveries warranted a large Other allocation. All three changed the numerical balance.

Orexin — 12%. The profile case rests on unusually clear defining discoveries, despite less exceptional career-wide impact comparisons. De Lecea's supplied hypocretin paper has disruption percentile 0.94 and foundation share 0.86; Yanagisawa's orexin/receptor paper supplies complementary foundational evidence. These are five-year measures for the respective papers, not career averages (supplied profiles). The external case is the strongest current-year convergence: the Lasker Foundation recognized Mignot and Yanagisawa for orexin, wakefulness and narcolepsy, while the FDA approved oveporexton on August 5, 2026, as a treatment directly targeting deficient orexin signaling. Approval is established; broad patient uptake is not inferred (2026 Lasker, FDA announcement). I read this as a strong discovery-to-disease-to-treatment package, tempered by the weak historical evidence for same-year award timing.

GLP-1 — 11%. Holst's inventive uptake and textbook reach are exceptional in the supplied comparisons. Mojsov contributes directly relevant hormone-processing and insulin-release work; Knudsen supplies therapeutic development. Their career-wide impact comparisons are less uniformly exceptional than those of several competing teams, which restrains the probability (supplied profiles). The external recognition is dense and recent: the Clinical Lasker recognized GLP-1 drug discovery and development, the Breakthrough Prize recognized the broader discovery-and-therapy chain, and Clarivate's September 17, 2026 announcement selected GLP-1 biology for Medicine recognition. Different award bodies selected different contributors, but that does not prevent a discovery match under this question (2024 Lasker, 2025 Breakthrough, 2026 Clarivate).

CAR-T — 9%. The supplied profiles show extraordinary translation and textbook establishment across June, Sadelain and Rosenberg. The record supports a broad interpretation of Chemistry. It does not support a rule that a field is “due,” or that adjacent subjects cannot win close together (official motivations).

I investigated four questions that could change the estimate: whether sequencing had a genuinely fresh recognition signal; whether Other contained concrete competitors; whether recent validation changed younger technologies’ readiness; and whether recognition of a scientist actually concerned the listed discovery.

Sequencing has the strongest listed recognition pipeline. The same trio received the 2022 Breakthrough Prize, announced on September 9, 2021, and the 2024 Gairdner recognition (Breakthrough announcement, Gairdner announcement). They then received the Asturias award on May 13, 2026, and Zurich’s Chemistry prize, announced on August 27, 2026 (Asturias jury, University of Zurich). The new Wolf Chemistry award recognizes the same discovery package. Its account explicitly connects surface DNA-colony amplification with Solexa’s sequencing-by-synthesis chemistry (Wolf Foundation). This is stronger than generic praise for genomics. I give the combined recognition a substantial uplift, but do not count each award as independent evidence of Nobel intent.

Other contains serious competitors, not just surprises. Clarivate’s September 17, 2026 Chemistry selections cover three unlisted discovery clusters: self-assembled monolayers, long-range protein electron transfer, and base/prime editing. Clarivate explicitly says its program does not predict recipients in a particular year (Clarivate announcement). The omissions have support beyond that list. Monolayers received the 2022 Kavli Nanoscience Prize; Liu’s editing technologies received the 2025 Breakthrough recognition; and modern systems proteomics received the 2026 Gairdner award (Kavli, Harvard, Gairdner). Buchwald–Hartwig amination and biomolecular condensates add distinct omitted contenders (Wolf citation, Clarivate’s earlier selections).

Recent validation strengthens several listed alternatives, but not enough to make them favorites. FDA approved the heterobifunctional protein degrader vepdegestrant on May 1, 2026. That removes the argument that targeted degradation lacks an approved therapeutic application; it does not establish decades of broad clinical impact (FDA). Perovskites received fresh Kyoto recognition, while the award’s own explanation still identifies operational lifespan and environmental challenges (Kyoto announcement, scientific citation). Langer’s May 19, 2026 Welch Chemistry award explicitly supports engineered-material delivery of therapeutic macromolecules, strengthening delivery’s Chemistry case rather than merely its biomedical importance (Welch Foundation).

The discovery-first model, A, divides each weight by their total, 108.5. The family model, B, uses the historical fractions 13/26, 5/26, 6/26 and 2/26. Within each family, it distributes that fraction in proportion to the listed weights plus an omitted-discovery reserve. My reserves are 15 for biological chemistry, seven for synthesis, 12 for materials and five for physical/theoretical chemistry. Controlled polymerization and C–H chemistry enter synthesis; perovskites, OLEDs and origami enter materials; molecular dynamics enters physical/theoretical chemistry; the remaining named options enter biological chemistry. Other receives the sum of the omitted allocations.

The final calculation is:

$$ p_i=0.75A_i+0.25B_i. $$

Here, A is the discovery-first probability and B is the historical-family probability. The blend favors current discovery-specific evidence without letting an attractive candidate list crowd out omitted fields. All allocation and blending coefficients are subjective. The JSON preserves the calculated distribution to six decimal places and sums exactly to one.

What's non-obvious

The Wolf announcement’s proximity to Nobel week makes it unusually salient, not unusually transparent. Reporting places the announcement on Thursday evening, October 1, before the client’s question (contemporaneous reporting). Under the Nobel’s published timetable, recommendations were due in September. The late Wolf announcement therefore supplies corroboration of scientific standing, not evidence that it caused, leaked or coordinated with the Nobel decision (selection timetable). This distinction prevents a strong signal from becoming an unjustified near-certainty.

Discovery matching also changes the forecast. Hartwig winning for carbon–heteroatom coupling would not automatically count as C–H functionalization: his joint Wolf citation with Buchwald concerns the former discovery (Wolf citation). Conversely, recognition of different pioneers of high-efficiency OLEDs can still match the listed OLED discovery (Clarivate’s OLED selection). I therefore separate discovery uncertainty from uncertainty about the laureate roster.

Uncertainties

The largest missing evidence is the committee’s actual candidate ranking and intended motivation. Confidential nominations and evaluations prevent direct verification. I also lack a validated same-year conversion model for precursor awards; correlated prize recognition cannot substitute for one (Nobel confidentiality rules, Clarivate’s methodological caveat). A broad or divided sequencing motivation could also require careful application of the client’s discovery and share rules.

The central estimate is sensitive to the omitted pool and the strength assigned to sequencing’s fresh awards. In calculation stress tests, I varied omitted weights and reserves by 25%, sequencing’s weight from 16.8 to 26.4, and the discovery-first blend from 60% to 90%. Sequencing ranged from 15% to 25%; Other ranged from 27% to 41.6%. These are model-sensitivity ranges, not calibrated confidence intervals. The stable conclusion is the ranking: sequencing leads the named discoveries, while Other remains the largest combined category. The decimal precision records the arithmetic, not equally precise knowledge of a secret decision.

Method

  • Same as Dashboard 1: the question text, options and resolution criteria (copied unchanged), the 101 cards and definitions notes (copied unchanged, treatment=consider, same seeded order), the Preseen client (unchanged copy), private questions, allow_incomplete_context=false, an idempotency key on every request.
  • Different: one extra note on the treat question, the instruction above, attached first with treatment=assume_true (the setting a premise note took effect with in the Mojsov check of 1 October); new question copies per arm because context notes apply to every later run of a question.
  • Runs: one control and one main-evidence run per field, submitted together on 2 October 2026 at about 09:05 CDT (Medicine, Physics, Chemistry in parallel), well before the announcements (5, 6 and 7 October). A read-back of the notes confirmed 0 notes on each control question and 1 + 31 / 35 / 35 on the treat questions, with no duplicates.
  • Fourth arm (one main source): new private copies of the same questions with the softer note (treatment=assume_true) and the same cards, one run per field, submitted together on 2 October 2026 at about 09:54 CDT; the client confirmed 1 + 31 / 35 / 35 notes before each run. No new control run: the five control runs are its baseline.
  • Yardsticks: because the main arm has one run, its distance from the control mean is compared with the same distance for single control runs (each against the mean of the other four) and for the single cross-check runs. The pairwise control spread of Dashboard 1 is kept in summary.json. Options are “outside the control range” when the run lies below the lowest or above the highest of the five control runs.
  • Direction: the Spearman correlation, over the twelve named options, between an option’s change and the laureate comparison printed on its people’s cards (impact; citing inventions as a second measure), as in Dashboard 1.

Caveats

  • One run per arm. A single main-evidence run per field; its distance from control is compared with single control runs, but the comparison is descriptive.
  • Two changes at once. The note both raises the cards and lowers outside evidence (awards, news). Without a placebo arm (the same note with the cards’ numbers shuffled across people) a move away from the award-driven ranking cannot be separated from following these particular numbers; the correlation with the laureate comparison is the check on the second.
  • A day apart. The baseline runs are from 1 October and the new runs from 2 October; the new control run measures the drift.
  • Card errors are kept. The cards are unchanged, including known weaknesses (e.g. a merged OpenAlex record for Robert Langer, thin profiles for Pascal Mayer, Rafi Bistritzer and Naoko Kurahashi Neilson), and the note asks the forecaster to take the figures as given.
  • Scope sentence. Asking to judge named options by their named people departs slightly from the resolution rule (resolution by discovery, whoever shares it); it keeps alternative rosters such as Drucker for GLP-1 from re-entering through the back door.
  • The fourth arm came later. The one-main-source note was written after the main-evidence results were seen, as a follow-up question (where does the forecast land between the two?); it is not a pre-planned dose arm, and its runs are about 50 minutes later than the main-evidence runs.
