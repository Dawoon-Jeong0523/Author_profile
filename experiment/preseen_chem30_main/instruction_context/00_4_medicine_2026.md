# Reference outcome: 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (announced 5 October 2026)

Awarded jointly, in equal shares, to Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel "for their discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and optogenetics". Hegemann and Nagel discovered channelrhodopsin, a light-gated channel protein, in a single-celled alga (papers of 2002 and 2003); Deisseroth used its gene to trigger nerve signals with light in rat neurons (published 2005) and in the brains of living mice (2007). Lag to the prize: about 23 years from the discovery papers, about 21 from the enabling method. The committee frames the award as a method that let neuroscience prove causal relationships and is now used in laboratories worldwide; clinical use (attempts to restore sight) is mentioned last, as an application. Earlier and parallel contributors named in the committee's background material but not awarded: Gero Miesenböck (first genetic light-sensitization of non-photoreceptor neurons, 2002) and Zhuo-Hua Pan (channelrhodopsin in retinal ganglion cells, published 2006); Miesenböck had shared most of the field's major prizes with Hegemann and Nagel. Before the announcement the most-tipped favourite was GLP-1 research, which carried the freshest major-prize cascade and the largest clinical impact; optogenetics had been on shortlists for years and was described as overdue once it won.
