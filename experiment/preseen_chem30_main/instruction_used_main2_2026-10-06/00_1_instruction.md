Assumption for this forecast: the profile notes attached to this question are the main evidence for comparing the named options. You are also given the outcomes of the 2026 Nobel Prizes in Physiology or Medicine (announced 5 October 2026) and in Physics (announced 6 October 2026), and a timing base rate for the Chemistry prizes of 2000-2025; use them only as described below.

- The profiles cover only the people named in the options. Judge each option by the profiles of its named people. A person named in two options counts fully for both on career measures; their profile note lists every option that names them. A defining work or a patent tied to the discovery counts for an option only if it is relevant to that option's discovery, and you state which works and patents you treated as relevant.
- Base the relative probabilities of the named options primarily on these profiles: the measures they report (impact, defining works, technological translation, textbook reach, collaboration) and the reference lines that compare each person with past laureates at the time of their prize. How to combine the people within an option is your judgment; state the rule you used. An option naming one person is not weaker for that reason alone.
- The measures are not equally informative. Career impact and discovery-relevant works are primary; textbook reach is supporting; collaboration is minor. Discovery-relevant works include the patents tied to the discovery that a profile lists. Read technological translation against the reference lines rather than as an absolute count: the Chemistry laureates of 2000-2025 had a median of about 200 citing inventions and 6 own US patents at prize time. Disruption percentiles are noisy and get little weight.
- Take the figures as given, with three exceptions you must state: you may down-weight a profile for visible attribution anomalies (an implausible publication window, affiliation or co-author list), for small samples (fewer than about 50 research works), or for works published after 2018, which have had little time to gather textbook and patent citations (their five-year impact windows are complete). Do not discount a profile because percentiles among past laureates are not calibrated selection probabilities; translating the profiles into probabilities is part of the task.
- The committee usually waits. Use the timing base rate as a prior on each option's maturity, measured as years from the awarded discovery and its first realization to 2026, from the years of the discovery-relevant works: options whose key works are under ten years old are competitive only if their adoption already matches the fast-award precedents. Apply this prior once, inside the profile-based ranking, not again as a recency discount later.
- Use the Medicine and Physics 2026 outcomes only to calibrate how a motivation pairs a specific discovery with the realization that made it a field-wide tool or a new observational window, how laureate credit followed the authorship of the decisive works and the leadership of the decisive instrument rather than conceptual priority or collaboration size (Physics went to one person alone), and how far the awards sat from the freshest recognition cascade. They are observations from other committees: they adjust weights at the margin and say nothing about which area of chemistry is due.
- Use other information (prizes and awards, recent news, published predictions, the history of the prize) only as a secondary adjustment, not as the main basis for the ranking: it may change an option's relative weight by a factor between 0.5 and 2, and every factor is listed with its option.
- Do not mix a uniform floor into the distribution; calibration is applied afterwards, identically to all arms. Probabilities over the listed options sum to 1.
- In the write-up, report the distribution from profiles alone (including the timing prior) and the distribution after secondary adjustment, which is the submitted forecast; state for each option above 5 % which profile evidence drove its probability; list the aggregation rule and every down-weighting and adjustment with its factor; and, as a secondary output, name the laureate set you would expect for each of the top three options from the authorship of the discovery-relevant works.
