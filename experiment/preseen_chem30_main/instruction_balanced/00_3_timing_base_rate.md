# Timing base rate (Chemistry prizes, 2000-2025)

Measured from the decisive work the motivation awards (the first realization or demonstration), approximately 15-20 % of the prizes had a lag of ten years or less, about a third a lag of 10-25 years, and about 45 % a lag of more than 25 years; the median is roughly 25 years. The short-lag cases were structures and tools adopted field-wide within a few years: the potassium-channel structure (1998 → 2003), the RNA polymerase II structure (2001 → 2006), ribosome structures (2000 → 2009), CRISPR-Cas9 genome editing (2012 → 2020) and AlphaFold protein structure prediction (2020 → 2024; it shared the prize with computational protein design, from 2003). Methods and materials waited: conductive polymers 23 years (1977 → 2000), organocatalysis 21 (2000 → 2021), click and bioorthogonal chemistry about 20 (2001-2004 → 2022), palladium-catalysed cross-coupling 31-42 (1968-1979 → 2010), cryo-electron microscopy about 27-42 (1975-1990 → 2017), the lithium-ion battery 34-43 (1976-1985 → 2019), quantum dots 30-42 (1981-1993 → 2023), metal-organic frameworks about 26-36 (1989-1999 → 2025). Lags are bimodal rather than normal. A prize may be divided between two discoveries (2024). The prize is not awarded posthumously, so very long lags carry a survival constraint (laureate aged 97 in 2019). These shares are approximate, counted from the official motivations and the years of the cited works.
