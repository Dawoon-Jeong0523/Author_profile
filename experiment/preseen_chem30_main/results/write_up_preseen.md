My forecast puts flexible organic electronics and electronic skin first at 8%, followed by synthetic gene circuits and theoretical methods for heterogeneous catalysis at 7% each ([calculation](sandbox:/mnt/data/chemistry_forecast_audit.json)). Those three discoveries account for 22% of the distribution, so the forecast supports spreading probability rather than backing one dominant favourite ([calculation](sandbox:/mnt/data/chemistry_forecast_audit.json)). These probabilities are conditional on the prize matching a listed discovery; they do not include unlisted outcomes or annulment ([resolution assumptions](sandbox:/mnt/data/chemistry_forecast_audit.json)).

## Evidence
The historical backbone is patience. In the supplied reference class of 26 Chemistry prizes awarded during 2000–2025, approximately 15–20% had a discovery-to-prize lag of ten years or less, about one-third had a lag of 10–25 years, and about 45% waited longer than 25 years. The median was roughly 25 years. The fast cases were enabling structures or tools with rapid, field-wide adoption—not simply highly cited new research ([supplied timing evidence](sandbox:/mnt/data/chemistry_forecast_audit.json)). I use that history as a maturity prior once. I do not impose a second youth penalty, reward a supposedly overdue subfield, or force these selected options to reproduce the historical bin shares.

The comparative evidence comprises 55 named researchers. The profiles cover articles, reviews, letters and patents through 2021, using the January 2026 OpenAlex snapshot and December 31, 2025 PatentsView vintage. Impact measures use complete five-year, publication-year-and-field-normalized citation windows. Their reference lines compare candidates with 61 Chemistry laureates from 2000–2025, measured before each laureate's prize. Patent and book measures are counts, not growth rates. Individual publication windows and sample sizes are retained in the audit; the supplied material gives no separate snapshot date for the patent-to-paper linking source ([supplied inputs](sandbox:/mnt/data/chemistry_forecast_audit.json)).

The model keeps career impact and discovery-specific evidence primary. Textbook reach supports them. Translation and collaboration have small weights. In particular, the supplied historical medians of approximately 200 citing inventions and six own US patents mean that modest-looking absolute patent numbers are not automatically weak ([reference data](sandbox:/mnt/data/chemistry_forecast_audit.json)).

**Aggregation and scoring.** I convert each supplied historical comparison rank to a fraction. A researcher's career score gives weights of 0.35, 0.30 and 0.35 to the reference ranks for median impact, top-decile share and top-centile share. Book support is the mean of the two book-reach ranks. Translation is the mean of the invention and patent ranks. Collaboration is the valid Nobel-coauthor count divided by three, capped at one. For each block separately, an option receives half the strongest named-person score and half the mean of its named-person scores. This does not add careers together or reward an option merely for naming more people. A singleton retains its own score ([scoring specification](sandbox:/mnt/data/chemistry_forecast_audit.json)).

For each relevant defining work, I combine its supplied impact percentile, foundation share and evidence role with weights of 0.50, 0.25 and 0.25. Evidence role is 1.00 for an original realization, 0.85 for an enabling contribution and 0.70 for a review. Missing foundation shares receive the neutral value 0.50. Partial discovery relevance multiplies that work's score. The option's discovery score is its strongest relevant work, not a sum of papers; repeated papers count once. Disruption and paper-level patent/book totals receive no numerical weight. That prevents a young paper's short downstream-citation window from becoming another maturity penalty ([work-level specification](sandbox:/mnt/data/chemistry_forecast_audit.json)).

The combined option score assigns 0.40 to career impact, 0.40 to discovery evidence, 0.12 to books, 0.06 to translation and 0.02 to collaboration. I exponentiate eight times that score and multiply by the maturity factor. After adding any secondary multiplier, I raise every arm's weight to the same power, 0.85, and normalize. The profile-only distribution uses the same calibration without secondary multipliers. There is no uniform floor. These coefficients are explicit forecasting judgments, not fitted selection probabilities ([complete calculation](sandbox:/mnt/data/chemistry_forecast_audit.json)).

**Maturity factors.** Established realizations older than approximately 25 years receive 1.00; the intermediate class receives 0.75. Glycan synthesis, coordination self-assembly and controlled delivery receive 0.875 because the supplied work lists place them near the boundary or establish only a review-based lower bound on age. Base/prime editing receives 0.35: its supplied evidence supports a fast-award tool case, but its realizations are only ten and seven years old. Skeletal editing receives 0.04: its directly relevant supplied realization is five years old, with much thinner evidence of adoption comparable to the fast-award precedents. External discovery-date checks do not trigger a second timing calculation ([timing inputs and factors](sandbox:/mnt/data/chemistry_forecast_audit.json)).

The supplied Medicine and Physics outcomes adjust how I read original realizations and leadership. They favour a discovery paired with the method or instrument that made it useful, and they undermine automatic three-person or fresh-prize-cascade bonuses. They produce no arm-specific multiplier and no inference about which chemistry area is due ([stipulated cross-committee observations](sandbox:/mnt/data/chemistry_forecast_audit.json)).

## What's non-obvious
The headline narrative centres on recognitions and familiar Nobel candidates. The requested evidence hierarchy produces a different ordering. Sequencing rises after the largest secondary correction, but its supplied named-person career comparisons remain weaker than those of the leading arms. Conversely, a large career cannot carry unrelated defining works into the discovery score: Bao's battery work, Wong's antibody work, Langer's tissue engineering and Hartwig's C–H borylation contribute no discovery-specific evidence to their respective listed options ([work filters and distributions](sandbox:/mnt/data/chemistry_forecast_audit.json)). Missing original papers deserve bounded repairs, not replacement rankings.

Discovery probability and laureate attribution are separate. For the leading electronic-skin option, I expect Bao, Stefan C. B. Mannsfeld and Darren J. Lipomi: the two original papers identify Bao's common leadership, Mannsfeld's joint conception of the pressure-sensor work and Lipomi's joint conception of the stretchable-sensor work ([2010 contribution statement](https://www.nature.com/articles/nmat2834); [2011 contribution statement](https://www.nature.com/articles/nnano.2011.184)). For synthetic circuits, I expect Collins, Elowitz and Leibler for a broad founding-field motivation; Gardner is the strongest alternative third-seat claim from the toggle-switch realization ([toggle-switch authorship](https://www.nature.com/articles/35002131); [repressilator authorship](https://www.nature.com/articles/35002125)). For surface-catalysis theory, I expect Nørskov and Bjørk Hammer; Jan Rossmeisl becomes the main alternative if the motivation centres on first-principles electrocatalysis rather than the broader electronic-structure framework ([RPBE authorship](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/files/4114802/Hammer.pdf); [electrocatalysis original](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/jp047349j)). These are attribution projections, not extra probability multipliers.

## Uncertainties
The largest gap is the committee's private evaluation of discovery boundaries and enabling realizations. The supplied three-paper lists are incomplete discovery histories. Deconflated career records, complete original-work lists and comparable evidence of field-wide adoption would sharpen the estimate most. The cross-committee observations cannot reveal Chemistry's choice, and outside recognition cannot close that gap ([evidence limits](sandbox:/mnt/data/chemistry_forecast_audit.json)).

A 20,000-draw parameter-sensitivity exercise varied career/discovery balance, concentration, common calibration, maturity factors and small score perturbations. Its central 90% ranges were approximately 5–11% for electronic skin, 6–9% for circuits and 6–9% for surface theory. These are model-sensitivity ranges, not statistically fitted confidence intervals; they omit additional uncertainty about private committee evidence and incomplete attribution histories ([sensitivity specification and results](sandbox:/mnt/data/chemistry_forecast_audit.json)). The JSON's decimal places preserve calculation precision, not predictive certainty.
