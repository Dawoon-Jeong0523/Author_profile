## TL;DR
Sequencing-by-synthesis is my first choice at 13%, followed by mass-spectrometry-based proteomics at 11% and self-assembled monolayers at 9% ([forecast calculation](#evidence)). These probabilities are conditional on the prize matching one of the listed discoveries, not on the named scientists being the exact laureates ([client resolution criteria](#context)). The leading option has an 87% probability of not being selected, so this remains an open race ([forecast calculation](#evidence)).

## Context
This forecast uses information available by October 6, 2026, at 23:59 UTC. The official announcement is scheduled for October 7 at 11:45 CEST, or 09:45 UTC, at the earliest; no Chemistry result enters the estimate ([official schedule](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/about/prize-announcement-dates/)). The client’s rules match discoveries rather than exact recipients, favor the most specific matching option, and annul the question if no option matches. I therefore normalize across the listed discoveries without estimating an “Other” outcome ([client resolution criteria](#context)).

The supplied profiles, timing reference, and Medicine and Physics outcomes are accepted inputs. The profile figures use the January 2026 OpenAlex snapshot, the December 31, 2025 patent vintage, and research and granted patents through 2021; their comparison panel contains 61 historical Chemistry laureates measured at prize time. Candidate-specific starting years and sample sizes remain those supplied on each card. I use these reference comparisons rather than current citation counters or raw career totals ([client-supplied evidence](#context)).

## Evidence
The historical backbone favors mature discoveries. The supplied reference covers all 26 annual Chemistry prizes from 2000 through 2025: approximately 15–20% had discovery-to-prize lags of ten years or less, roughly one-third had lags of 10–25 years, and about 45% exceeded 25 years, with a median near 25 years. These are approximate shares of awarded discoveries, not annual winning probabilities for candidates ([supplied timing reference](#context); [official prize history](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/lists/all-nobel-prizes-in-chemistry/)). I use them as readiness evidence, not as a waiting-time clock that makes an old discovery automatically due.

Prior prizes establish boundaries, not blanket exclusions. The earlier mass-spectrometry award recognized soft-ionization methods, while the proteomics option concerns identification, quantification, and interpretation at scale. The earlier genome-editing award makes another closely related editing prize harder to select so soon. Exchange-correlation functionals face particularly close overlap with the already rewarded density-functional framework. These are selection discounts, not claims that the later work lacks scientific importance ([2002 motivation](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2002/summary/); [2020 announcement](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2020/press-release/); [1998 announcement](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1998/press-release/)).

The stipulated Medicine result supplies a useful warning: a mature enabling method beat the freshest clinical-impact narrative. The stipulated Physics result shows that a discovery can have a clear central contributor despite a large collaboration. I take both as reasons to avoid overvaluing recent publicity or penalizing singleton options. They do not establish a cross-committee rotation rule ([client-supplied reference outcomes](#context)).

**Sequencing-by-synthesis leads because its discovery-specific evidence closes much of its whole-career profile gap.** Balasubramanian’s supplied record contains 358 works from 1958–2021, and Klenerman’s contains 326 from 1986–2021. Their general impact comparisons are less exceptional than those of several rivals. But the reversible-terminator genome paper, sequencing arrays, labelled nucleotides, and associated patents are directly relevant; their invention-reach comparisons exceed 97% and 92% of the supplied reference panel, respectively ([supplied profiles](#context)). The original genome-scale demonstration establishes a working chemical measurement architecture, not merely commercialization of an existing sequencer ([original experimental paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature07517)).

Its external recognition is the strongest in this comparison. The Asturias jury’s May 13, 2026 decision recognizes Balasubramanian, Klenerman, and Mayer for the widely used sequencing technologies. The University of Strasbourg’s October 4 announcement confirms the same trio’s Wolf Chemistry award before the cutoff. The earlier Gairdner recognition also identifies the complementary sequencing and DNA-cluster contributions ([Asturias decision](https://www.fpa.es/en/princess-of-asturias-awards/laureates/2026-david-klenerman-shankar-balasubramanian-pascal-mayer/?texto=acta); [dated Wolf confirmation](https://isis.unistra.fr/en/news/pascal-mayer-laureat-du-prix-wolf-2026-de-chimie/); [2024 Gairdner account](https://www.gairdner.org/resource-hub/2024-canada-gairdner-award-winners-announced)). Attributed pre-announcement commentary adds support, but I distinguish a specific sequencing-by-synthesis endorsement from a broader endorsement that also includes nanopores ([Chemistry World discussion](https://www.chemistryworld.com/podcasts/nobel-prize-in-chemistry-2026-predictions/4024287.article); [C&EN panel discussion](https://cen.acs.org/people/nobel-prize/predictions-2026-nobel-prize-chemistry/104/web/2026/10)).

**Proteomics is the strongest profile-based challenger.** The supplied records cover Yates’s 1,014 works from 1945–2021, Mann’s 797 from 1972–2021, and Aebersold’s 788 from 1982–2021. Mann and Aebersold combine exceptional impact, invention reach, and textbook comparisons; Yates adds directly relevant database-search and shotgun-proteomics inventions. I exclude Yates’s unrelated microglia paper from discovery-specific evidence ([supplied profiles](#context)). The March 31, 2026 Gairdner announcement explicitly unifies the trio around scalable measurement, quantitative reliability, and computational interpretation. That makes this a coherent enabling discovery rather than an award for three productive careers ([official Gairdner announcement](https://www.gairdner.org/fr/publication/laur%C3%A9ats-du-prix-gairdner-du-canada-2026)). Its reservation is that the prize would encompass several interconnected methods, and the earlier mass-spectrometry Nobel already discussed biological identification and medical applications ([2002 announcement](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2002/press-release/)).

Self-assembled monolayers combine strong profiles with a mature, chemically clear discovery. The supplied records cover Allara’s 236 works from 1965–2021, Nuzzo’s 403 from 1975–2021, and Whitesides’s 1,505 from 1962–2021. Whitesides contributes exceptional invention and textbook reach; Allara and Nuzzo contribute the directly relevant surface-assembly and characterization work. Whitesides’s microfluidics work does not count as direct monolayer evidence ([supplied profiles](#context)). The Kavli award explicitly recognizes the discovery, and the current Clarivate selection adds corroboration. Sagiv’s inclusion in those recognition sets creates no exact-recipient penalty under this question’s rules ([Kavli citation](https://www.kavliprize.org/prizes/nanoscience/2022); [Clarivate selections](https://clarivate.com/citation-laureates/chemistry/)).

The next group remains competitive for different reasons. Genetic-code expansion combines Chin’s strong impact comparisons with Schultz’s exceptional invention and textbook reach, supported by relevant orthogonal-translation patents. Schultz’s supplied record covers 678 works from 1980–2021 and Chin’s 136 from 1969–2021; the Welch announcement provides independent recognition of expanding biochemical building blocks ([supplied profiles](#context); [Welch announcement](https://www.scripps.edu/news-events/news/20250520-schultz-welch-award/)). Base and prime editing have one of the strongest individual profiles: Liu’s supplied record contains 193 works from 1992–2021, with impact and translation comparisons near the top of the reference panel. Direct Breakthrough recognition supports the inventions, but their short lag and adjacency to the earlier editing prize reduce this year’s selection weight ([supplied profile](#context); [Breakthrough account](https://www.broadinstitute.org/news/david-liu-receives-breakthrough-prize-life-sciences); [earlier editing prize](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2020/press-release/)).

Buchwald–Hartwig coupling and controlled radical polymerization have mature, discovery-linked patent and translation evidence. The supplied Buchwald and Hartwig records contain 998 works from 1976–2021 and 717 from 1986–2021; the Matyjaszewski and Sawamoto records contain 1,261 from 1973–2021 and 389 from 1975–2021. I separate Hartwig’s amination evidence from his C–H chemistry, and distinguish the founding polymerization work from later reviews ([supplied profiles](#context)). Joint Wolf recognition supports the coupling case, while joint Franklin recognition supports independent polymerization discoveries. Their reservations concern neighboring Nobel territory and the breadth of controlled-radical lineages, not practical importance ([coupling award](https://news.mit.edu/2019/buchwald-awarded-wolf-prize-chemistry-0116); [polymerization award](https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2016/october/matyjaszewski-franklin-medal.html)).

Chaperones, carbohydrate synthesis, nanopores, and dye-sensitized cells retain substantial weight. Hartl’s fresh recognition strengthens the chaperone case; Wong has discovery-specific chemistry recognition; and the foundational nanopore experiment establishes a distinct sensing lineage rather than another version of sequencing-by-synthesis ([Keio account](https://www.ms-fund.keio.ac.jp/en/prize/the-2026-keio-medical-science-prize-awardees.html); [Wong award](https://www.scripps.edu/news-events/news/20210914-wong-receives-robert-welch-award-chemistry/); [original nanopore experiment](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.93.24.13770)). Conversely, Caruthers and Suntola receive maximal defining-invention grades despite modest general-impact comparisons: consequential patents are genuine discovery evidence ([supplied profiles](#context); [Gray recognition](https://clarivate.com/citation-laureates/chemistry/); [technology-prize history](https://millenniumprize.org/winners/)).

The numerical rule gives profiles and other evidence equal coefficients. Within the profile grade, citation impact receives 20%, relevant defining works and patents 30%, technological translation 25%, textbook reach 20%, and collaboration 5%. Impact averages the three supplied reference comparisons for each person; a multi-person option receives 60% of its strongest individual score plus 40% of its named-person mean. The other dimensions are discovery-level judgments using the same strongest-contribution-plus-corroboration principle. A singleton receives no headcount penalty. Missing disruption values are not zero, and unrelated landmark works do not strengthen the defining-discovery grade ([supplied definitions and profiles](#context)).

I calculate relative weights and then apply the same uncertainty tempering to every arm:

$$
w_i=L_iM_i\exp\left(0.09(P_i+E_i)\right),\qquad
p_i=\frac{w_i^{1/1.20}}{\sum_j w_j^{1/1.20}}.
$$

Here L is the readiness adjustment: 0.60 for base/prime editing, 0.85 for the perovskite-containing options, 0.90 for targeted degradation and the kinase/KRAS option, and 1 otherwise. M is the motivation-specificity adjustment: 0.12 for combined dye/perovskite cells, 0.20 for combined DNA synthesis/sequencing, and 1 otherwise. These are judgmental adjustments, not fitted historical hazards. The common tempering softens concentration without adding a uniform floor. The final decimal probabilities use exact-sum rounding; the extra digits document normalization, not empirical certainty ([forecast calculation](#evidence)).

## What's non-obvious
Proteomics’ profile advantage is real, but it does not settle the prize. The question rewards a discovery, not the best whole-career bibliography. Sequencing-by-synthesis has unusually direct links from molecular chemistry and patents to a widely adopted measurement platform. Proteomics has an equally serious claim through an integrated toolkit. Also, Gairdner recognition is not unique to the proteomics challenger: the sequencing trio already received it earlier. The comparison therefore turns on discovery coherence and attributable impact, not simply which field received the latest award ([original sequencing paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature07517); [proteomics citation](https://www.gairdner.org/fr/publication/laur%C3%A9ats-du-prix-gairdner-du-canada-2026); [sequencing citation](https://www.gairdner.org/resource-hub/2024-canada-gairdner-award-winners-announced)).

**Recipient mismatch is not outcome mismatch.** Mayer, Sagiv, Winkler, or Langer can strengthen a discovery’s external case without appearing on its supplied profile cards. Their omission is not an automatic probability penalty. The opposite applies to the combined options: adding Caruthers or Grätzel to a possible recipient set does not itself make a sequencing-only or perovskite-only motivation resolve to the broader arm. That matching rule is why both combined options receive much less weight than their strong constituent discoveries ([client resolution criteria](#context)).

## Uncertainties
The largest missing evidence is the Academy’s private assessment of the discoveries and its chosen motivation. Nominations, investigations, and related opinions remain confidential for 50 years. Public awards and expert commentary establish scientific plausibility; they do not reveal the final shortlist ([official selection process](https://www.nobelprize.org/nomination/chemistry/)). Fresh award announcements also need not have influenced a committee whose recommendations were prepared earlier in the selection cycle ([official timetable](https://www.nobelprize.org/nomination/chemistry/)).

The conversion from profiles and external evidence into selection weights remains judgmental. Across nine specifications varying the profile share from 40% to 60% and the effective concentration coefficient from 0.065 to 0.085, sequencing ranges from 9% to 19%, proteomics from 9% to 14%, and monolayers from 8% to 9%. Those are model-sensitivity ranges, not validated confidence intervals, and the top two can exchange places ([sensitivity calculation](#evidence)). A historical, out-of-sample test of discovery-specific scoring would improve calibration more than additional live citation totals.

Finally, broad or divided motivations can make discovery matching consequential. The client’s share, ordering, and specificity rules determine those cases; unlisted discoveries are excluded by conditioning rather than assigned zero unconditional probability ([client resolution criteria](#context)). I do not infer that an option lacks support merely because it receives little press coverage, and neither market prices nor reader votes enter this calculation ([forecast method](#evidence)).
