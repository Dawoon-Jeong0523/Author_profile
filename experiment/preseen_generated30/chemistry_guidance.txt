Generate the options yourself: exactly 30 options and no "Other" option.

Each option is one discovery or invention, written as one line in the style of an official Nobel
motivation ("for the development of ...", "for the discovery of ..."), followed by " — " and the
people you expect to share the prize for it, most credited first:
  <discovery> — <Name>[, <Name>[, <Name>]]

Who to name
- Name only as many people as you think the Nobel committee would actually choose: one, two or three.
  Do not fill three places by default. Name a single person when one person is clearly central to
  the discovery, two when two are.
- Every person must be alive on 7 October 2026; the prize is not awarded posthumously. Check recent deaths.
- Use full names as commonly published; no affiliations.

Which discoveries
- Choose the 30 discoveries you consider most likely to receive the 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry,
  from any area of chemistry, including biochemistry, materials, theory and methods.
- Leave out discoveries already rewarded by a Nobel Prize in any field, and narrow follow-ups of them.
- Options must be mutually exclusive. Each discovery appears once. Do not list a broader and a
  narrower version of the same work as separate options (for example a technology and one of its
  components, or two generations of the same device); merge them into one option.
- Name a person in two options only if they are central to two clearly distinct discoveries.

Return only the 30 options in the format above, with no extra text.
