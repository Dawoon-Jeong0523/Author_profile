## Evidence
The historical backbone is the supplied timing reference class: the 26 annual Physics prizes from 2000–2025. Approximately 15–20% followed the decisive realization within ten years, about 35% had a 10–25-year lag, and about 45–50% waited longer; the supplied median is roughly thirty years. Fast awards rewarded dramatic observations or enabling tools. These are accepted, approximate premises. They are not candidate-level selection rates, because the number of eligible discoveries in each age band is unknown ([supplied timing evidence](sandbox:/mnt/data/nobel_physics_evidence.md)).

I therefore apply a damped maturity prior once: a relative factor of 1.00 for realization lags of 10–25 years and 1.10 for older anchors. Magic-angle graphene receives 0.45 for its short experimental lag. The black-hole shadow receives 0.75 because a dramatic first observation resembles the supplied fast-award precedents more closely. The supplied Medicine outcome contributes only a small shift toward discovery-plus-realization and field-wide scientific enablement. It gives no physics area a turn-taking bonus and supplies no separate option multiplier ([timing and Medicine treatment](sandbox:/mnt/data/nobel_physics_evidence.md)).

The main evidence remains the client’s profiles. Their stated vintages are OpenAlex January 2026 and PatentsView 31 December 2025, with publication and patent-filing windows ending in 2021. Across the 33 named people, research samples range from nine to 1,020 works; the historical reference lines compare them with 62 Physics laureates awarded during 2000–2025. Five-year impact measurements are complete. Book and patent measures are cumulative counts, with more elapsed accumulation time for candidates than for some historical reference laureates. I accept these figures as instructed, rather than replacing them with newly retrieved bibliometrics ([profile provenance and units](sandbox:/mnt/data/nobel_physics_evidence.md)).

The aggregation rule is 75% of the named people’s mean score plus 25% of their strongest score. This rewards both team depth and a central contributor without automatically favoring three-person options. A shared landmark paper counts once. Career impact uses the supplied historical reference ranks, weighted 40% median impact, 35% top-decile share and 25% top-percentile share. Reliability exceptions shrink a person’s composite toward the reference midpoint; they do not multiply that person’s ultimate selection probability ([aggregation rule](sandbox:/mnt/data/nobel_physics_evidence.md)).

Discovery evidence gives each relevant paper 85% weight on impact percentile and 15% on Foundation share. When both anchors exist, the anchor score is 40% prediction and 60% realization. The discovery-paper component is then 75% anchors and 25% supporting works. Missing metrics receive the within-option median of available relevant metrics, not zero. I retain the supplied low-impact optical-clock precursor rather than silently replacing it with a better-scoring paper. Disruption receives no separate numerical weight ([paper scoring and imputations](sandbox:/mnt/data/nobel_physics_evidence.md)).

A small coherence rubric completes the discovery score: 80% paper evidence and 20% discovery-narrative coherence. Coherence is 1.00 for topological insulators, clocks, simulators, cascade lasers and electron optics; 0.95 for metamaterials, quantum information and the black-hole shadow; and 0.90 for phases, magic-angle graphene, neutrinos and inflation. These are declared judgments about the supplied contribution chains, not additional timing or empirical-confirmation penalties ([coherence inputs](sandbox:/mnt/data/nobel_physics_evidence.md)).

The overall score assigns 50% to career impact, 35% to discovery evidence, 10% to book reach, 3% to technological translation and 2% to collaboration. The Medicine analogy moves two percentage points from career to discovery evidence; it does not change the ranking through an area-specific factor. Book reach uses equal weights on cited works and distinct citing books. Technology uses 75% citing inventions and 25% own patents. Counts are logarithmically scaled and capped at 400 cited works, 3,000 citing books, 200 inventions and eight patents. Collaboration gives equal weight to the presence of a Nobel-laureate coauthor and a capped logarithmic coauthor–coinventor count ([complete scoring specification](sandbox:/mnt/data/nobel_physics_evidence.md)).

The probability conversion is:

$$
W_i=M_i\exp(9S_i),\qquad p_i^{\mathrm{profile}}=\frac{W_i^{0.85}}{\sum_j W_j^{0.85}},\qquad p_i^{\mathrm{final}}=\frac{(W_if_i)^{0.85}}{\sum_j(W_jf_j)^{0.85}}.
$$

Here, S is the weighted profile score, M is the maturity factor, and f is the bounded secondary adjustment. The common power calibration is identical across all options. There is no uniform floor. The parameters are judgmental rather than fitted Nobel-selection coefficients ([calculation audit](sandbox:/mnt/data/nobel_physics_evidence.md)).

## What's non-obvious
The profiles favor quantum simulation before any recent recognition enters the model. Its advantage comes from sustained strength across the named team, not one spectacular paper or one high-scoring researcher. Conversely, the enormous patent portfolios attached to metamaterials and cascade lasers are weak discriminators once scaled against the supplied laureate reference class. Those portfolios support importance; they do not substitute for discovery-specific evidence ([primary-score comparison](sandbox:/mnt/data/nobel_physics_evidence.md)).

The printed laureate sets are not reliable shortcuts to discovery probabilities. Relevant paper authorship can support a different recipient set, while the option still resolves YES. I therefore exclude unrelated landmarks from discovery scoring, retain the named people’s careers as instructed, and keep person-credit forecasts separate. I also avoid treating the supplied Medicine result as evidence that a particular physics field is due ([resolution and attribution treatment](sandbox:/mnt/data/nobel_physics_evidence.md)).

## Uncertainties
The largest unknown is the committee’s comparative judgment, not scientific change during the remaining hours. The model also lacks the population of eligible discoveries in each maturity band and definitive individual-contribution statements for multi-author anchors. Those gaps limit how sharply career strength, waiting time and authorship can be translated into selection probabilities ([model limitations](sandbox:/mnt/data/nobel_physics_evidence.md)).

Across nine variants that change career/discovery weights and the score-to-probability slope, quantum simulation remains first, with a probability ranging from 20% to 36.2%. That is a model-sensitivity range, not a statistical confidence interval. The JSON’s decimal places preserve arithmetic and normalization; they do not imply corresponding precision about a secret committee choice ([sensitivity audit](sandbox:/mnt/data/nobel_physics_evidence.md)).
