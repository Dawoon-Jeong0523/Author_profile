**How the forecaster reasons (both arms).** The runs rank discoveries by the breadth of past Chemistry awards and the
recognition record of each discovery, treat awards as correlated evidence of one achievement, and keep the forecast
dispersed: no named discovery approaches an odds-on favourite.

**“Other” is the largest single outcome in every run (32–35 %).** The write-ups name concrete omitted contenders rather than
generic uncertainty: Clarivate's 2026 Chemistry selections (self-assembled monolayers, long-range electron transfer in
proteins, base and prime editing), the 2022 Kavli Prize for self-assembled monolayers, Buchwald–Hartwig carbon–nitrogen
coupling, and recent Gairdner awards (systems proteomics, cryo-electron tomography). Several of these were just outside
the committee's top twelve (self-assembled monolayers 13th, electron transfer in proteins 18th, Buchwald–Hartwig coupling
19th after it was split from C–H functionalization); base and prime editing were not nominated at all.

**Sequencing-by-synthesis leads the named discoveries in every run (18–20 %).** It combines mature practical impact with
repeated recognition of the same discovery, most recently a new Wolf Prize in Chemistry. The runs calibrate that signal
with a small reference class: of 18 Wolf Chemistry cohorts, only two (2001 and 2022) contained a same-year Chemistry
laureate, so the Wolf Prize is read as a standing signal, not a timing detector. A stress test in one run moves the
sequencing forecast between 13 % and 23 % depending on how much the new award is worth. Perovskite solar cells (about 8 %)
and controlled radical polymerization (about 7 %) follow.

**What the cards changed.** The treat subforecasts refer to the supplied bibliometrics about eleven times per run (control:
none) and again use them as secondary evidence. The ranking is stable (Spearman 0.96); five small options move just
outside their control ranges (molecular chaperones +1.0 point, targeted protein degradation +0.5, nanopore sequencing
+0.4, C–H functionalization −0.6, DNA origami −0.3), while the largest single change, perovskite solar cells −1.1
points, stays within its control range. The shifts are not related to how the options' people compare with past
laureates on impact (exploratory correlation 0.01).
