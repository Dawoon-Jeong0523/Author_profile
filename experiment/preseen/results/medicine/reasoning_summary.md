**How the forecaster reasons (both arms).** The question resolves on the discovery named in the official motivation, so
every run ranks discoveries rather than people. It weighs the strength and recency of outside recognition (Lasker,
Breakthrough Prize, Clarivate Citation Laureates and similar awards), how well a discovery fits a Nobel motivation
(fundamental physiology that has led to benefit for patients), and discounts options whose credit is spread over many
contributors. Awards are explicitly treated as correlated assessments of one achievement, not as independent signals
to be multiplied, and the runs repeat that public awards do not reveal the committee's confidential shortlist.

**GLP-1 leads in every run (17–23 %).** Its hormone-to-treatment pathway is described as the strongest cumulative case on
the list: the 2024 Lasker Clinical Award, the 2025 Breakthrough Prize and Clarivate's 2026 selection, joining endocrine
physiology to large clinical benefit (including the cardiovascular indication of semaglutide). The main discount is
attribution: several groups contributed, although a different trio of laureates would still resolve to this option.

**Orexin is second (11–14 %),** on fresh physiological and therapeutic validation (a new basic-science award and direct
therapeutic validation of the sleep–wake pathway).

**“Other” is the largest single outcome (29–35 %).** The runs argue that the list omits serious contenders, naming DNA
sensing (cGAS–STING), optical coherence tomography and the targeted treatment of acute promyelocytic leukaemia, and that
even the strongest named option still loses about four times in five.

**What the cards changed.** In the treat runs the subforecasts add a passage on “the supplied bibliometrics”: they are
used as a secondary cross-check and deliberately not converted into likelihood ratios, because percentiles among past
laureates are not calibrated selection probabilities, author disambiguation is imperfect and there are no unsuccessful
candidates for comparison. The ranking logic is otherwise unchanged. The small shifts point the way the cards' laureate
comparisons do (our reading, exploratory): optogenetics, whose lead person Karl Deisseroth is above 98 % of past laureates
on impact, gains 1.4 points and rises three places; GLP-1, whose first-named person Svetlana Mojsov is above 27 %, loses
1.4 points, within the range of the control runs.
