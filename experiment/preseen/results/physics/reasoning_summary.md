**How the forecaster reasons (both arms).** As in Medicine, every run ranks discoveries by how compact and well recognised
the discovery is, treats public prizes as evidence of scientific stature rather than of the committee's confidential
choice, and spreads probability widely: no named discovery is allowed a dominant share.

**“Other” is the largest single outcome in every run (29–35 %),** because the menu omits several recognised families of
discoveries; the runs stress that “Other” stands for many omitted candidates, not for one stronger rival.

**Optical lattice clocks lead the named discoveries in every run (14–16 %).** The write-ups call them the strongest compact
discovery case: the Breakthrough Prize for their invention and development, and a Wolf Prize announced on 1 October 2026
that recognises Jun Ye (clocks) and Immanuel Bloch (optical-lattice quantum simulation); the runs split that update
between the two options rather than crediting it to clocks alone, and note that the redefinition of the SI second has not
happened yet.

**Topological insulators follow (8–11 %), then negative-index metamaterials (7–9 %).** A Nobel Symposium on metamaterials is
read as strengthening that field's case without identifying the winner. Quantum information and quantum simulation
receive a small timing discount for adjacent recent prizes (entanglement in 2022, macroscopic quantum tunnelling in
2025), while the runs warn against treating adjacent science as already rewarded.

**What the cards changed.** The treat subforecasts again discuss the supplied bibliometrics as secondary evidence (about
eight references per run, against 0.5 in control) without turning them into odds. The changes are small: “Other” falls
by 1.6 points (within the control range), aberration-corrected electron optics gains 0.9 points and is the one clear move
outside the control range (cosmic inflation, −0.2 points, sits exactly at the lower edge of its control range), and
optical lattice clocks and quantum simulators gain about 0.8 points each, within their control ranges. Unlike Medicine, the shifts are not related to how the options' people compare with past laureates on their
cards (exploratory correlation 0.07).
